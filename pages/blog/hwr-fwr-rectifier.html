Study of HWR and FWR Using CT Transformer

Jan 29, 2023 15 min read

Part 1: Study of Half-Wave Rectifier

Theory

The half-wave rectifier is a simple circuit that converts AC voltage into a pulsating DC voltage. It consists of a diode connected in series with the load resistor. The diode conducts only during the positive half-cycle of the AC voltage.

Disadvantages: Low efficiency and high ripple factor.

Part 2: Study of Full Wave Rectifier Using Center Tapped Transformer

Theory

A full-wave rectifier converts the whole AC input voltage into DC voltage. It uses two diodes and a center-tapped transformer. During the positive half-cycle, one diode conducts, and during the negative half-cycle, the other diode conducts.

Advantages:

  • Smoother output waveform.
  • Higher efficiency compared to half-wave rectifier.
  • Lower ripple voltage.

Conclusion

The full-wave rectifier provides a more stable, efficient, and steady DC voltage compared to the half-wave rectifier. It is the preferred choice for applications where a constant DC voltage is required.
