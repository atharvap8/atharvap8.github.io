Testing of Diodes and Transistors

Jan 29, 2023 10 min read

Theory

Diode Testing

A diode is a two-terminal electronic component that allows current to flow in one direction only. It acts like a one-way valve for current in a circuit.

  • Forward Bias: Anode connected to positive, cathode to negative.
  • Reverse Bias: Anode connected to negative, cathode to positive.

To test a diode, use a multimeter to measure forward and reverse resistance. Forward resistance should be low; reverse resistance should be infinite.

Transistor Testing

A transistor is a three-terminal component used as a switch or amplifier. The most common type is the BJT.

To test a transistor, measure the current gain (hFE) using a multimeter. The gain should typically be in the range of 100 to 1000.

Procedure

  1. Connect the diode/transistor to the breadboard.
  2. Set the multimeter to the appropriate testing mode (diode or transistor).
  3. Measure forward/reverse resistance for diodes or current gain for transistors.
  4. Compare with standard values to determine working status.

Conclusion

Testing diodes and transistors is an important step in the design and maintenance of electronic circuits. By understanding the basic principles and procedures of testing, we can ensure the proper functioning of the components.
