Basics of Electrical & Electronics - Unit 1 & 2

February 12, 2025 Electronics Basics

Unit 1: Elementary Concepts

1. Concept of Potential Difference, Current, and Resistance

  • Potential Difference (Voltage, V): Difference in electric potential between two points. Measured in Volts (V).
  • Current (I): Flow of electric charge. Measured in Amperes (A).
  • Resistance (R): Opposition to current flow. Measured in Ohms (Ω).

2. Ohm’s Law

Voltage is directly proportional to current when resistance is constant.

V = I × R

3. Series and Parallel Circuits

Series Circuit

  • Single path for current.
  • Total Resistance: R_t = R_1 + R_2 + ... + R_n
  • Current remains same in all components.
  • Voltage divides across components.

Parallel Circuit

  • Multiple paths for current.
  • Total Resistance: 1/R_t = 1/R_1 + 1/R_2 + ... + 1/R_n
  • Voltage remains same across components.
  • Current divides among branches.

4. Voltage and Current Dividers

Voltage Divider Rule: V_x = V_total × (R_x / R_total)

Current Divider Rule: I_x = I_total × (R_total / R_x)

5. Power and Energy Calculations

  • Power (P): Rate of energy transfer. Measured in Watts (W). P = V × I
  • Energy (E): Total power used over time. Measured in Joules (J). E = P × t
  • Electricity Bill Calculation: Energy is measured in Kilowatt-hours (kWh). Cost = Units Consumed × Rate per kWh.

6. Kirchhoff’s Laws

Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL)

Sum of currents entering a junction = Sum of currents leaving the junction.

Σ I_in = Σ I_out

Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL)

Sum of all voltages in a closed loop = 0.

Σ V = 0

Unit 2: Single Phase and Polyphase A.C. Circuits

1. Generation of Single Phase Sinusoidal A.C. Voltages

Alternating Current (AC) varies sinusoidally over time and is generated using rotating coils in a magnetic field.

AC Voltage Equation: v(t) = V_m sin(ωt)

  • V_m = Peak Voltage
  • ω = Angular Frequency
  • t = Time

2. AC Quantities and Phasor Representation

  • Phasor: Represents AC quantities as rotating vectors in a complex plane.
  • Amplitude (Magnitude): Peak value of voltage or current.
  • Phase Angle: Difference in phase between voltage and current.

3. Pure R, Pure L, and Pure C Circuits

  • Pure Resistive Circuit: Voltage and Current are in phase. Power P = V × I.
  • Pure Inductive Circuit: Current lags voltage by 90°. Power P = 0 (purely reactive).
  • Pure Capacitive Circuit: Current leads voltage by 90°. Power P = 0 (purely reactive).

4. Impedance and Admittance

Impedance (Z): Total opposition in an AC circuit. Measured in Ohms (Ω). Z = R + jX where X is reactance.

Admittance (Y): Reciprocal of impedance. Measured in Siemens (S). Y = 1/Z

5. Active, Reactive, Apparent Power and Power Factor

  • Active Power (P): P = V × I × cos φ
  • Reactive Power (Q): Q = V × I × sin φ
  • Apparent Power (S): S = V × I
  • Power Factor (PF): PF = cos φ

PF close to 1 → Efficient system. PF close to 0 → High reactive power, inefficient system.

6. Polyphase A.C. Circuits

Three-phase systems are used for efficient power transmission, providing more power with less conductor material compared to single-phase.

Balanced Three-Phase System: Equal phase voltages and 120° phase difference.

Relation between Line and Phase Quantities

  • Star Connection: V_L = √3 V_ph, I_L = I_ph
  • Delta Connection: V_L = V_ph, I_L = √3 I_ph

7. Power in Three-Phase Circuits

Power is same for both Star and Delta connections:

P = √3 V_L I_L cos φ
